Khan Expert Field Engineer App

A single-role Expo application for field technicians servicing AC and appliance call tickets — replacing the browser-based Field Engineer Portal with an offline-tolerant native app.

Scope Engineer role only Stack Expo SDK 57 · expo-router Backend Existing Express + MongoDB Target Android-first
01

What we are building, and why it is not a port

The CRM already has a Field Engineer Portal at /engineer/assigned-calls. It is a responsive web page that lists assigned call tickets and opens a drawer to update them. It works, but it assumes a desk: a stable connection, a keyboard, a file picker, and a browser session the technician keeps alive.

Field reality is different. A technician stands on a rooftop next to a condenser unit, reads a serial number off a label in bad light, photographs the installation, records what they did and what they collected, and moves to the next address. Connectivity is intermittent. The phone is mid-range Android. The session must survive a day without re-login.

So this is a rebuild around four things the web portal cannot do well: camera capture with on-device compression, an offline write queue so an update made in a basement is not lost, push notification of new assignments, and a session that persists in secure storage rather than localStorage.

Everything else — billing, purchases, masters, ledger, inventory, leads, user management — stays in the web CRM and is out of scope here. That is what makes this app small enough to be good.

02

What the engineer actually does today

Read from EngineerCallEditDrawer.tsx and engineerCallController.ts, the current engineer workflow is narrow and well-defined. The app must reproduce exactly this, not more:

  1. Log in with phone or email plus a password set for them by an administrator.
  2. See their assigned, still-open tickets. Closed tickets are hidden from the engineer entirely.
  3. Open a ticket and read customer name, contact and WhatsApp numbers, address with pincode, route, brand, product, model, indoor and outdoor unit serial numbers, warranty terms and expiry, service type, and any notes left by office staff.
  4. Enter the machine's serial number, read off the label on the back or bottom of the unit.
  5. Set a sub-status — In Progress, Attended, Pending Parts, Job Done, Work Completed, Customer Rescheduled, Customer Refused.
  6. Add line items for work performed: a service name such as Gas Charging or PCB Repair, plus an amount and optional remarks.
  7. Record the amount collected from the customer.
  8. Attach service photographs.
  9. Write free-text visit remarks; supply a mandatory reason if the job was refused or cancelled.

Rules the server enforces, which the UI must mirror

These are real constraints in updateEngineerCallStatus. The app should express each of them in its interface rather than letting the technician discover them through a rejected request:

  • An engineer cannot close or cancel a ticket directly. Attempting either returns 403.
  • Once a ticket reaches Job Done, Completed, Work Completed or Closed, it is locked to the engineer. No further edits.
  • Primary jobStatus is writable only by office staff; the engineer writes subStatus. Marking a sub-status containing "job done" or "work completed" promotes the primary status server-side.
  • A cancellation or refusal requires a reason, or the request is rejected with 400.
  • Every engineer update appends an entry to the ticket's activityLogs — this is an audit trail the office reads.

Design consequence: the update screen is a form with a locked state, not a free editor. Render the lock as a read-only view with an explanation, disable the two forbidden statuses rather than listing them, and make the reason field appear and become required the moment a refusal status is chosen.

The technician's job ends at "what I did and what I collected." The Release 2 wallet changes what the engineer sees about their own earnings; it still shows them nothing about the customer's invoice.

05

Backend work this app requires first

The mobile app will lean on the engineer endpoints far harder than the web portal ever did — more requests, worse networks, retries, and a long-lived token on a device that can be lost. Four defects in the current engineer path become materially worse under that load. These are prerequisites, not nice-to-haves.

B1

Engineers are identified by name, not by ID

Blocker

Assigned tickets are scoped with { engineer: engineerName }, and the call document stores engineer as a plain string. Two technicians sharing a name see and edit each other's work. Renaming an engineer in the master silently detaches their entire history — on a phone, that presents as every ticket vanishing at once.

Add engineerId to the call schema, backfill it by name, scope all engineer queries by it, and keep the name field for display only.

backend/src/controllers/engineerCallController.ts:8, :90 · models/calldetailsModel.ts
B2

Searching silently drops the closed-ticket filter

Blocker

In getAssignedEngineerCalls the filter sets $or for the closerDate condition, then overwrites that same key with the search $or. The moment the technician types in the search box, the exclusion of closed tickets disappears and closed work reappears in the list. The web portal masks this by re-filtering on the client; the app should not have to.

Move both conditions into an $and array.

backend/src/controllers/engineerCallController.ts:19–36
B3

The engineer routes have no permission guard

Blocker

engineerCallRoutes.ts applies only authenticateJWT. The ownership check inside the controller fires only when the role is literally engineer, so any other authenticated account — including a staff user holding no permissions at all — can edit any call ticket through this route, bypassing calls:edit.

Restrict the router to the engineer role and make the ownership check unconditional.

backend/src/routes/engineerCallRoutes.ts · controllers/engineerCallController.ts:113
B4

Uploaded temp files are never deleted

Blocker

express-fileupload runs with useTempFiles: true writing into ./tmp/, and fs.unlink appears nowhere in the backend. Every photo already leaks a file onto the server disk. An app that encourages three or four photos per visit across a field team will fill that disk, and there is no MIME or extension validation on the way in either.

Delete the temp file in a finally block after the Cloudinary upload, and validate type and size before accepting.

backend/src/index.ts:63–70 · controllers/calldetailsController.ts (uploadServiceImage)
B5

Login is unthrottled and the token lives 30 days

High

express-rate-limit is installed and applied to the public website-lead endpoints, but neither login route is throttled. Engineer tokens are issued for 30 days with no refresh mechanism and no server-side revocation, so a lost phone is a lost account until an administrator deactivates the engineer.

Rate-limit both login routes, and pair a short access token with a refresh token the app can rotate — or at minimum ship the change-password endpoint from section 04 so a compromised account can be recovered by its owner.

backend/src/routes/engineerAuthRoutes.ts · controllers/engineerAuthController.ts:38
B6

The list endpoint refetches every assigned ticket on every request

High

Alongside the paginated query, getAssignedEngineerCalls loads all of the engineer's open tickets a second time purely to compute four summary counts. On a phone polling for updates this is the single most expensive call in the app.

Replace the second query with an aggregation, and add the ?since= delta parameter so routine refreshes transfer almost nothing.

backend/src/controllers/engineerCallController.ts:47–72

B1 through B4 should land before the app's first field test. B5 and B6 can follow during the beta, but not after general release.

Offline model

Reads are cache-first: the ticket list and each opened ticket are written to SQLite and served from it immediately, then revalidated. A technician who opens the app in a lift sees yesterday's list rather than a spinner.

Writes go into a durable queue, each entry holding the ticket ID, the payload, a client-generated idempotency key, an attempt count and a state. A background task drains the queue whenever connectivity returns, in submission order. Photos queue as local file URIs and upload separately, so a large image cannot block a status update.

Two rules keep this honest. First, the queue is visible — see section 06. Second, the server is always the winner on conflict: if a ticket was locked or closed by the office while the technician was offline, the queued write is rejected, and the app surfaces that as a clear message naming the ticket, rather than retrying forever or discarding it silently.

Idempotency needs a server counterpart. PUT /api/engineer-calls/:id appends to activityLogs on every call, so a retried request writes a duplicate audit entry. Either honour a client idempotency key, or make the app's retry strategy strictly at-most-once for updates that may have partially succeeded.

08

Non-functional requirements

  • Devices. Android-first, mid-range and older. Assume 3 GB RAM and a 720p screen as the floor. iOS only if technicians actually carry iPhones — confirm before spending on it.
  • Network. Every screen must have a defined state for offline, slow, and failed. No infinite spinners.
  • Photographs. Resize and compress before upload; target well under 1 MB per image against the server's 10 MB cap.
  • Battery and data. Poll sparingly and prefer push. Delta sync over full refetch once ?since= exists.
  • Language. The localization/ folder exists for a reason — Bengali and Hindi are realistic needs for a West Bengal field team. Decide early; retrofitting strings is expensive.
  • Accessibility. Large tap targets and high contrast. The app is used outdoors, in sunlight, sometimes with gloves.
  • Distribution. EAS Build. Internal distribution APK for the beta; Play Store only if the team wants managed updates.

10

Decisions needed before P1

Five questions change the Release 1 build. The rest I can decide as I go. Release 2 carries nineteen more, catalogued in docs/specs/11-open-questions.md — including the commission model and the Aadhaar retention question, both of which need someone with business authority rather than a developer.

Is offline support in the first release, or a fast follow?

P3 is a quarter of the total effort. Shipping online-only first gets the app into hands two weeks sooner, at the cost of updates lost in basements and lift shafts. Recommend including it — for a field team it is the reason to have an app at all.

Android only, or iOS too?

iOS adds Apple Developer enrolment, review cycles and device testing. Recommend Android-first unless technicians already carry iPhones.

Bengali or Hindi, or English only?

Cheap to build in from the start, expensive to retrofit. Needs a real answer about who uses the app, not an assumption.

Who fixes the backend blockers?

P0 is backend work in a separate repository with its own deployment. If that is someone else's, it needs scheduling now, because P2 depends on it.

Should engineers see closed tickets at all?

Today the server hides them completely. Technicians often want yesterday's work for reference — a read-only history tab is small, but it is a product decision and a new query.
